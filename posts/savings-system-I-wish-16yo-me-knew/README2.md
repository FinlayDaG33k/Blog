# Savings system I wish 16y/o me knew

You hear it everywhere, people having issues paying their bills, going in debts just to make it to the end of the month.  
Yet, when you then look, those same people indulge in many things considered "luxury goods".  
From expensive take-out food, to festivals, to expensive matcha latte's.
I used to be in a fairly similar boat for a long time, except it was cameras, networking equipment, computer parts and later, car parts.
While I didn't really go into debt either, finances became very tight at times.  

Most people would just stick to a simple and naive way of saving, which often isn't robust and still leaves you open for a lot of problems when an unexpected bill arrives.  
Again, I used to be within this same boat for a long time, until I developed my current savings system, which has many benefits and fail-safes.  
The only thing it requires, is some discipine, especially in the start as you'll have to go full-on saving for a bit.

## Naive Way

The naive way is the way pretty much everyone learns: You set a simple goal (eg. a new phone) and you put money into that until you can afford it. 
This is also how I learned to save from a fairly young age and how my savings worked until mid-2025.
But this has a few problems that I've encountered when I grew up and life was no longer "just buying games":

- How to decide how much you save each time?
- When do you save?
- What happens when an unexpected bill comes?

Many people do not really know the answers to these, which is why I call it the "naive way" of saving.  

- Deciding how much you save really depends on your income and your bills, which makes it hard to give a fixed amount.  
- Deciding when to save is easy, "when my pay comes in"... But then they generally won't have enough for the bills.
- When an unexpected bill comes, that means digging into the phone funds, which kind of defeats the point.
