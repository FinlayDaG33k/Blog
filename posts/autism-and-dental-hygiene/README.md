# Autism and dental hygiene

As someone that has been diagnosed with autism, I often disliked brushing my teeth.
In part because it just doesn't fit well in my routines, requiring a "dedicated moment", rather than easily being able to do things concurrently, but also because the products I used were deterring me from doing it.
While I luckily am not very prone to cavities, it did cause my gums to recede more than they should have.
In the long term, this will cause sensitivity as well as likely cause premature loss of teeth.

**NOTE**: Autism can be very broad and doesn't affect everyone the same way.  
As a result, this is not 100% guarranteed to work.  
This is simply what worked to help improve the situation for me and my partner.

## Change your toothpaste

Toothpaste (or toothgel, I call them both the same) is often the first problematic thing when it comes to dental hygiene.  
A lot of toothpaste aimed at general adults have a strong minty flavour and smell.  
However, it was this exact strong mintyness that made me dislike it since it lingers too long (often for hours), so I wanted to try something less minty.  
The problem is that this generally leaves me at "children's toothpaste", especially if I want to be able to just walk to the store and get a new tube, rather than having to scour the internet to order something.  
However, since the needs of children are _generally_ different than those of _adults_, formulations may differ in key points.  
In order to not cause too much more problems where I can help it, I had to first figure out what makes them different beyond the flavour.

### Adult Toothpastes Evaluated

First, let's evaluate what goes inside an Adult toothpaste so I can have a baseline to work with.  
I have gathered some of the toothpastes my parents use here at home, as well as some toothpastes I have used in the past (but never finished the tube - but for some reason kept) or ones that I keep handy.  
This got me the following list of toothpastes:

* Elmex Anti-Caries Menthol Free (used by Mom).
* Sensodyne Sensitive (used by Dad).
* Sensodyne Sensitive Extra-Fresh (used by me in some circumstances).
* Prodent Complete Protection (sampler tube I found in the drawer on the bathroom).
* Theramed 2-in-1 Original Fresh (my old tooth paste).
* Blue M (old tooth paste, recommended by my dentist years ago).

You can find the full list of ingredients per toothpaste here [here](toothpaste-ingredients-adult.ods) (ingredients ordered to make it easier to compare).  
Looking at the list, I found two noteworthy ingredients were often used and what they were used for.  

The first one was `Hydrated Silica`, which was used in all formulations.  
Its main purpose is as an abrasive to help with the removal of dental plaque, as well as gently polish the tooth surface (to get that smooth texture on your teeth).

The second one was `Sodium Fluoride`, which was lacking in the Blue M formulation, which is a fluoride carrying compound used to strenghten the enamel.  
This is the stuff that can help prevent cavities and sometimes even help teeth remineralize itself.  
It is also why brushing before bed is the most important moment and why you shouldn't "rinse" with water after having brushed your teeth (you'll just flush away the fluoride).  
In the four formulations that _did_ have it, the concentration was between 1425 and 1450 parts per million of fluoride.

Next I noticed `Potassium Nitrate`, while only being part of the Sensodyne formulations, does serve a significant purpose on that it numbs the nerve cells of the tooth, helping alleviate sensitivity.  
Sensitive teeth are (often) caused by receding gumlines exposing the root of the teeth.  
As a result of this, children's toothpaste _generally_ don't have this as they shouldn't have this problem yet.  
This is also why I still sometimes use `Sensodyne Sensitive Extra-Fresh` despite it tasting horribly minty.
Some alternative compounds I have found while researching are: Arginine, Calcium Carbonate and Strontium Chloride, which block the tubules (the "gateway" to the nerve cell) themselves.

`Xylitol` is also an interesting one, found in the Blue M formula.  
It is a sweetening agent that also is known to aid in reducing cavities by reducing growth of `Streptococcus mutans` and `Streptococcus sobrinus` in the mouth (the ones that convert sugary stuff to acids that break down the enamel).  
This is caused by the bacteria absorbing the Xylitol as if it was sugar but since they cannot get energy from it, they instead die from "exhaustion".  
And as it turns out, dead bacteria don't do well in breaking down enamel.  
And before you start chewing on pure Xylitol crystals, there is no strong evidence that suggests Xylitol on its own really helps.  
While I have not dug deep into the research surrounding it, my speculation would be that the reduced bacterial growth combined with the enamel strenghtening effects of fluoride are what decreases cavities.  
A case of "build faster than they can break it down".  
`Sorbitol` is similar to Xylitol, except it can be used by bacteria to create acids and energy (just to a significantly lesser degree than sugar).

So to summarize we want in terms of "active ingredients":
- We want a Fluoride concentration of between `1425 ppm` and `1450 ppm`.
- Preferably Xylitol to slow down bacterial growth.
- Possibly have some `Hydrated Silica` to help remove plaque.
- Maybe Potassium Nitrate, Arginine, Calcium Carbonate or Strontium Chloride?
